# Fleet Purchasing Policy for Spares and Stores (PUR-POL-02)

**Version:** 4
**Department:** Technical Operations / Procurement
**Effective Date:** Immediate

## Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the standardized framework governing the procurement of spares, stores, and consumables for the company’s fleet. The primary objective is to ensure that vessels receive necessary materials in a timely manner while maintaining strict financial controls and operational efficiency. This document applies to all company-owned and managed vessels, as well as the shore-based technical and procurement teams responsible for fulfilling these requirements.

Adherence to this policy ensures consistency in supplier selection, cost management, and inventory control across all regions of operation. It defines the responsibilities of key stakeholders, including the Chief Engineer, Technical Superintendent, Purchase Manager, and Fleet Director, to prevent unauthorized expenditures and ensure accountability at every stage of the purchasing lifecycle.

## Requisitions

The procurement process initiates with the creation of a requisition on the designated purchasing system. The Chief Engineer is solely responsible for raising these requisitions, ensuring that all requested items are accurately described, quantified, and aligned with the vessel’s immediate operational needs. Accuracy in the initial request is critical to prevent delays or the receipt of incorrect items.

Upon submission, the requisition undergoes a mandatory review by the Technical Superintendent. This review serves as the first line of quality control, verifying that the requested items are necessary, correctly specified, and compliant with the vessel’s approved inventory lists. The Technical Superintendent ensures that the requisition aligns with budgetary constraints and operational priorities before it proceeds to the approval workflow. Only after this review is complete does the requisition move to the appropriate approval stage based on its total order value.

## Approval Limits

Financial authority is delegated according to the total order value in USD to ensure appropriate oversight. The following hierarchy governs all procurement activities:

- **Up to USD 1,000**: Approval authority rests with the Technical Superintendent. This tier covers routine consumables and minor replacements that do not require higher-level intervention.
- **USD 1,000 to USD 10,000**: These orders require dual approval from both the Technical Superintendent and the Purchase Manager. This joint authorization ensures that mid-range expenditures are scrutinized for technical necessity and commercial viability.
- **Above USD 10,000**: High-value procurement requires final approval from the Fleet Director. This level of oversight is necessary for significant capital outlays or bulk purchases to ensure alignment with fleet-wide strategic goals and budget allocations.

All approvals must be recorded within the purchasing system prior to the issuance of any purchase order. No goods may be procured without explicit digital authorization corresponding to the correct approval tier.

## Quotations

To ensure competitive pricing and value for money, the policy mandates specific quotation requirements based on order value:

- **Orders above USD 10,000**: Must include at least 3 competitive quotations from approved suppliers. This requirement ensures robust market comparison and prevents overpayment for high-value items.
- **Orders between USD 1,000 and USD 10,000**: Require at least 2 competitive quotations. This balance allows for reasonable price verification while maintaining operational agility for medium-sized purchases.

Quotations must be obtained from suppliers who are currently active in the company’s approved vendor list. The Purchase Manager is responsible for validating that the quotations are comparable in terms of specification, delivery terms, and warranty conditions before they are submitted for approval.

## Critical Spares

Inventory management for critical components is strictly regulated to prevent operational downtime due to part unavailability. Items marked as "critical" in the official catalogue are subject to minimum stock level requirements. These spares must never fall below a minimum stock of 2 units on board or in the warehouse.

The Technical Superintendent and Chief Engineer must monitor stock levels for these items regularly. If inventory drops toward this threshold, automatic alerts should trigger a replenishment requisition regardless of the current order value limits, provided the procurement follows the standard approval and quotation protocols unless an emergency is declared. Maintaining this buffer ensures that immediate replacements are available during unexpected failures, safeguarding vessel safety and schedule integrity.

## Emergency Purchases

In situations where operational safety or regulatory compliance is at risk, emergency purchases may be authorized to bypass the standard quotation process. This exception allows for expedited procurement when time sensitivity precludes the collection of competitive bids.

However, this flexibility is balanced by strict post-facto controls. Emergency purchases must be approved by the Fleet Director within 24 hours of the initial request. Furthermore, the purchaser must document a detailed reason for invoking the emergency protocol, justifying why standard procedures could not be followed. This documentation is subject to audit to ensure that the emergency clause is not misused for routine procurement failures or poor planning.

## Delivery Planning

Effective logistics coordination is essential to minimize vessel downtime and avoid demurrage costs. Orders must be placed with sufficient lead time to account for supplier processing and transit durations. Specifically, the order must be placed at least the supplier lead time plus 7 days before the vessel's arrival at the delivery port.

This buffer period allows for contingency handling of delays, customs clearance issues, or last-minute specification changes. The Chief Engineer is responsible for forecasting these dates accurately in the requisition, and the Technical Superintendent must verify that the proposed delivery date aligns with the vessel’s itinerary. Failure to adhere to this planning window may result in unnecessary port calls or extended laytime, which are avoidable through proactive scheduling.

## Supplier Selection by Region

To optimize logistics costs and leverage regional expertise, the company utilizes preferred suppliers based on geographic location:

- **Europe**: SUP-01 is the designated preferred supplier for vessels operating in European waters.
- **South-East Asia and Sri Lanka**: SUP-02 holds the preferred status for this region.
- **Middle East and India**: SUP-03 is the authorized preferred supplier for these areas.

While preferred suppliers are utilized to streamline procurement, they must still comply with all quotation requirements outlined in the policy unless an emergency exemption applies. Deviations from preferred suppliers require documented justification in the purchasing system.

## Receiving and Inspection

Upon arrival of goods at the vessel or warehouse, a formal inspection process is mandatory. Goods must be inspected on receipt to verify that they match the purchase order in terms of quantity, specification, and condition. Any discrepancies, including damage, wrong items, or shortages, must be reported to the supplier within their returns window.

The Chief Engineer or designated storekeeper is responsible for conducting this inspection and documenting any issues with photographic evidence and written reports. Timely reporting is crucial to facilitate refunds, replacements, or claims. Failure to report discrepancies within the stipulated window may result in the company bearing the cost of defective or incorrect goods.

## Records

Accurate record-keeping is vital for financial auditing, inventory analysis, and supplier performance evaluation. All purchase records, including requisitions, approvals, quotations, purchase orders, delivery notes, and inspection reports, must be retained for 7 years.

These records are maintained in the central purchasing database and are accessible to authorized personnel for audit purposes. Regular reviews of these records help identify trends in spending, supplier reliability, and inventory turnover, informing future policy adjustments and strategic procurement decisions.

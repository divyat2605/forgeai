# Business Travel and Expense Policy

**Policy Number:** FIN-POL-04  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Effective Date:** Immediate  
**Applicability:** Shore Staff of Kestrel Shipmanagement

## Scope

This policy establishes the standards, guidelines, and procedures for business travel and expense management for all shore-based employees of Kestrel Shipmanagement. The objective is to ensure that travel activities are conducted efficiently, cost-effectively, and in compliance with internal financial controls while maintaining safety and professionalism. All shore staff planning or undertaking business-related travel must adhere strictly to the provisions outlined herein. Failure to comply may result in the denial of reimbursement or disciplinary action. This policy applies to all domestic and international travel authorized by the company.

## Travel Booking and Mode of Travel

Employees are required to utilize the designated corporate travel management system for booking flights, trains, and other transportation modes. The mode of travel is determined primarily by the distance of the trip to balance cost efficiency with employee comfort and time management.

For domestic trips over 500 km: air travel in economy class. Trips of 500 km or less: train in AC 2-tier. Employees must select the most direct route available unless a deviation is explicitly approved for operational reasons. First-class or business-class upgrades are not permitted under any standard circumstance. If no direct train service exists for shorter distances, air travel may be considered only with prior written justification and approval from the department head, subject to budget availability.

International travel bookings must be made through the corporate travel agent to leverage negotiated rates. Employees are expected to book economy class tickets exclusively. Any request for premium seating or lounge access must be pre-approved in writing by the CFO, demonstrating a clear business necessity that outweighs the additional cost.

## Accommodation

Hotel accommodations must be booked through the approved corporate hotel partners where possible to ensure compliance with negotiated rates and simplified billing processes. When booking outside of partner networks, employees must ensure the selected property meets safety and hygiene standards.

The company enforces strict nightly limits on accommodation costs based on the location of the business trip. Hotel limits per night: INR 6,500 in metro cities (Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Hyderabad); INR 4,000 in all other cities. These limits are inclusive of taxes and service charges. If a hotel room exceeds these limits due to limited availability during peak seasons or major events, the employee must seek prior approval from their line manager. Such approvals will be granted only if the rate difference is justified by proximity to the business venue or safety concerns. Excess costs not covered by prior approval will be borne by the employee.

## Daily Allowance

To simplify expense reporting and cover incidental costs such as meals, local transportation, and miscellaneous expenses, the company provides a daily allowance (per diem). This allowance is intended to replace the need for submitting individual receipts for minor food and transport items, provided the total daily spend does not significantly exceed the allowance.

Daily allowance: INR 1,200 per day for domestic travel; USD 60 per day for international travel. The allowance is calculated based on the number of full days spent on business travel. For partial days, the allowance is prorated according to the time spent away from the regular duty station. Allowances are not paid for days where meals or transportation are provided by the host company or client. Employees must declare any such provisions in their expense claim to avoid double-dipping.

## International Travel

International travel involves additional logistical and financial considerations, requiring stricter advance planning and higher-level authorization. All international travel needs a travel request at least 21 days before departure and approval from the CFO. This lead time allows for proper visa processing, flight booking at optimal rates, and budget allocation.

Employees must submit a comprehensive travel itinerary along with the request, including estimated costs for flights, accommodation, and daily allowances. The CFO will review the business justification, budget impact, and necessity of the trip before granting approval. Late requests submitted without valid emergency justification may be rejected or subject to higher booking costs, which the employee may need to cover personally if no exception is granted.

## Travel Advances

In certain cases, employees may request a travel advance to cover initial expenses such as visa fees, deposits, or immediate cash needs upon arrival. The company permits a Travel advance: up to 70% of the estimated trip cost. The "estimated trip cost" must be calculated using current exchange rates and confirmed quotes for flights and accommodation.

Advances are disbursed only after the full travel request has been approved. Employees are responsible for settling any outstanding advances before submitting new expense claims. Unsettled advances will block the processing of future reimbursements until the previous balance is cleared. Any unused portion of the advance must be returned to the finance department within five business days of returning from the trip.

## Expense Claims

All expenses incurred during business travel must be documented and submitted for reimbursement through the company’s digital platform. Expense claims must be submitted in the KestrelHR portal within 30 days of returning; receipts are required for every item above INR 500. Claims submitted after this deadline will not be processed unless accompanied by a valid explanation for the delay, approved by the department head.

Receipts must be original, legible, and clearly show the date, vendor name, items purchased, and total amount paid. Digital receipts are acceptable if they meet these criteria. For expenses below INR 500, a detailed log entry in the portal is sufficient, but employees should retain physical or digital proof of purchase for audit purposes. All claims must be categorized correctly (e.g., accommodation, meals, transport) to facilitate accurate accounting and budget tracking.

## Approvals

The approval workflow for expense claims is tiered based on the total value of the claim to ensure appropriate oversight. Approval of claims: the line manager approves claims up to INR 50,000; claims above INR 50,000 also need the department head. The line manager is responsible for verifying that the expenses were incurred for legitimate business purposes and comply with this policy.

The department head reviews high-value claims to ensure alignment with departmental budgets and strategic objectives. Both approvers must verify that all necessary receipts are attached and that the claim form is complete. Any discrepancies, missing documentation, or policy violations identified during the review process will result in the rejection of the claim until rectified. Repeated non-compliance may lead to revocation of travel privileges.

## Personal Vehicle Use

In situations where public transportation or rental cars are unavailable or impractical, employees may use their personal vehicles for business travel. To ensure fairness and accountability, the company reimburses mileage at a standardized rate rather than actual fuel and maintenance costs. Use of a personal car for business is reimbursed at INR 12 per km.

Employees must record the starting point, destination, and total kilometers traveled in their expense claim. The route taken should be the most direct and efficient path between locations. Odometer readings or GPS logs may be requested during audits to verify the accuracy of the claimed distance. Personal vehicle use for commuting between home and regular office is not covered under this policy; it applies only to travel between business sites or client locations.

## Ship and Port Visits

Special protocols apply when shore staff visit vessels or port facilities, as these environments present unique safety and security requirements. Safety is the paramount concern during such visits. Ship visits: the company issues personal protective equipment (PPE); a port pass is arranged through the port captain at least 2 working days before the visit.

Employees must ensure that all issued PPE is in good condition and worn correctly during the visit. Failure to wear appropriate PPE may result in immediate denial of entry to the vessel or port area. The port pass is mandatory for access to restricted areas; visiting without a valid pass is strictly prohibited and may result in legal consequences or permanent ban from the facility. Employees must coordinate with the port captain well in advance to ensure all documentation is processed correctly. Any delays in pass issuance are the responsibility of the employee to anticipate and plan for, not an excuse for non-compliance.

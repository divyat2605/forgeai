# Standard Operating Procedure: Enclosed Space Entry

## Scope

This procedure establishes the mandatory safety protocols for all personnel entering enclosed spaces aboard the vessel. An enclosed space is defined as any area with limited means of entry or exit that is not designed for continuous human occupancy, including but not limited to cargo holds, ballast tanks, fuel oil tanks, void spaces, and pump rooms.

The primary objective is to prevent fatalities and injuries resulting from atmospheric hazards, entrapment, or mechanical failure during entry. This protocol applies to all crew members, contractors, and visitors who may require access to such areas. No individual is permitted to enter an enclosed space without strict adherence to the steps outlined in this document. Violation of these procedures constitutes a serious breach of safety regulations and will result in immediate disciplinary action.

## Permit to Enter

Access to any enclosed space is strictly controlled through a formal Permit to Enter system. The issuance of this permit is the sole responsibility of the Master, who retains ultimate authority over all entry operations. Before a permit can be signed, the Officer in Charge must verify that all preparatory safety measures have been completed and validated.

The permit serves as legal authorization for entry and must be physically signed by the Master prior to any personnel crossing the threshold into the space. The validity of this permit is strictly limited to one shift of maximum 8 hours. If the work extends beyond this timeframe, or if the shift changes, the previous permit must be cancelled, and a new permit must be issued following a fresh assessment of conditions.

The permit document itself must clearly state the specific location being entered, the nature of the work to be performed, the names of all personnel entering, and the time of issuance. It is imperative that the permit remains visible at the entrance of the space for the duration of the entry. If any condition changes or if an emergency occurs, the permit is immediately voided until a new assessment is conducted.

## Atmosphere Testing

Atmospheric testing is the critical first step in ensuring the safety of entrants. Before any ventilation begins, initial testing must be carried out to establish baseline conditions. The space must be ventilated for at least 30 minutes before testing to ensure that stagnant air pockets are cleared and that fresh air has circulated throughout the volume.

Testing must be conducted using calibrated, multi-gas detectors capable of measuring oxygen, combustible gases, hydrogen sulfide, and carbon monoxide simultaneously. Samples must be taken at the top, middle, and bottom of the space, as different gases have varying densities and may stratify. Testing should occur immediately before entry and continuously during the operation if portable monitors are used.

The atmosphere is only considered acceptable for entry if it meets all of the following criteria simultaneously:
- Oxygen levels must be exactly 20.8% by volume. Levels below this indicate potential asphyxiation risks, while levels above may indicate oxygen enrichment hazards.
- Hydrocarbon concentrations must remain below 1% LFL (Lower Flammability Limit). Any reading above this threshold requires immediate ventilation and re-testing.
- Hydrogen sulfide (H2S) levels must be below 5 ppm. H2S is highly toxic and can cause rapid unconsciousness even at low concentrations.
- Carbon monoxide (CO) levels must be below 25 ppm. CO is odorless and deadly, requiring strict monitoring.

If any parameter falls outside these acceptable limits, entry is prohibited until corrective action restores the atmosphere to safe levels. Ventilation must continue, and re-testing must confirm safety before a permit can be signed.

## Attendant and Communication

A dedicated attendant must remain at the entrance of the enclosed space at all times while personnel are inside. The attendant’s primary role is to monitor the entrants and maintain communication; they are strictly prohibited from entering the space under any circumstances unless authorized by a specific rescue protocol. The attendant must maintain constant visual or verbal contact with the entrants.

Communication checks must be performed every 10 minutes. These checks serve to confirm that all entrants are conscious, aware of their surroundings, and in good health. If communication is lost for any reason, or if an entrant reports feeling unwell, all personnel must exit the space immediately, and the permit must be suspended.

The attendant must possess a fully charged two-way radio or other reliable communication device compatible with the vessel’s network. They must also have immediate access to the emergency alarm system. The attendant is responsible for logging the time of each communication check on the permit document. This log provides a critical record of the entrants' status throughout the duration of the work.

## Rescue

Preparedness for rescue operations is non-negotiable. The vessel must maintain a dedicated enclosed space rescue team that is trained and equipped to respond to emergencies within these confined areas. This team must have access to appropriate breathing apparatus, harnesses, tripods, and winches suitable for the specific geometry of each enclosed space.

To ensure readiness, enclosed space rescue drills are held every 2 months. These drills simulate realistic scenarios, including unconscious entrants and equipment failure, to test the response time and effectiveness of the rescue team. Drills must be debriefed afterward to identify gaps in procedure or equipment maintenance.

In the event of an emergency, the attendant must immediately raise the general alarm and notify the bridge. The Master will initiate the vessel’s Emergency Response Plan. Rescue operations must only proceed if the rescuers are protected from the same hazards that endangered the entrants. Unprotected entry by rescuers is strictly forbidden due to the high risk of multiple fatalities.

## Records

Accurate documentation is essential for regulatory compliance and operational continuity. All completed permits to enter enclosed spaces must be retained on board for 3 years. These records serve as evidence of compliance with safety standards and provide a historical log of hazardous area usage.

The permit file must include the signed original permit, the atmospheric test results recorded at the time of issuance, and the attendant’s log of communication checks. Any incidents or near-misses associated with an entry must be documented in the vessel’s official logbook and referenced in the permit file. Regular audits by the Safety Officer will verify that records are complete, legible, and stored securely for the required retention period.

# Shipboard SMS Procedure: Incident and Near-Miss Reporting

## Purpose and Scope
This procedure establishes the mandatory protocols for reporting safety-related events aboard the vessel. The objective is to ensure immediate containment of hazards, accurate data capture for root cause analysis, and continuous improvement of maritime safety standards. Strict adherence to these guidelines is required for all crew members, regardless of rank or department.

## What to Report
The scope of reportable events is comprehensive and non-negotiable. All injuries, spills, near misses and equipment failures affecting safety must be reported. This includes minor first-aid cases that do not require off-hire medical attention, as well as significant environmental releases. Any malfunction of critical navigation, propulsion, or fire-fighting systems constitutes a reportable equipment failure, even if the vessel remained operational. Near-misses, defined as events where no injury, damage, or loss occurred but had the potential to do so, are equally critical to document. The goal is total transparency; if an event impacts safety, it must be logged.

## Immediate Actions
Upon occurrence of any reportable event, the Officer of the Watch (OOW) or responsible department head must prioritize life safety and environmental protection. Secure the scene to prevent further harm or evidence contamination. If medical assistance is required, initiate emergency protocols immediately. For spills, deploy containment booms or absorbents as per the Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan (SOPEP).

For serious incidents, such as those involving significant injury, major pollution, or total loss of propulsion, immediate verbal notification is mandatory. Serious incidents are reported to the Designated Person Ashore (DPA) by phone immediately. This call must provide a concise summary of the event, current status, and any immediate risks. Simultaneously, preserve all relevant data, including VDR records, engine logs, CCTV footage, and witness statements. Do not alter or clean up the scene until authorized by the DPA or company management, unless safety dictates otherwise.

## Reporting Timelines
Timely reporting is essential for effective analysis and regulatory compliance. The following strict timelines apply to all crew members:

*   **Serious Incidents:** In addition to the immediate phone call, a detailed written report must be submitted within 24 hours. This document should include chronological events, initial assessment, and preliminary corrective actions taken.
*   **Near-Misses:** Near-miss reports are submitted within 72 hours in the reporting system. These reports should focus on the sequence of events and contributing factors rather than blame.
*   **General Incidents:** All other reportable events, including minor injuries and equipment failures, must be logged in the vessel’s internal safety management system within 48 hours of occurrence.

Delays in reporting without valid justification will result in disciplinary action. The Master is responsible for ensuring that all reports are accurate, complete, and submitted on time.

## Investigation
Investigations are conducted to identify root causes and prevent recurrence. Investigations of serious incidents start within 48 hours. A joint investigation team comprising the Master, Chief Engineer, and Safety Officer will be formed for serious incidents. For near-misses, the department head may conduct a preliminary investigation, which is then reviewed by the Safety Officer.

The investigation process involves:
*   Reviewing all collected evidence and witness statements.
*   Analyzing operational procedures and maintenance records.
*   Identifying gaps in training, equipment, or communication.
*   Developing corrective actions with assigned responsibilities and deadlines.

Findings must be documented in the incident report file. Corrective actions are tracked until closure to ensure effectiveness.

## Lessons Learned
The value of reporting lies in the dissemination of knowledge. Each vessel must submit at least 2 near-miss reports per month to maintain a proactive safety culture. These reports, along with serious incident findings, are reviewed by the company’s Safety Department. Key lessons learned are distributed to all vessels via Safety Bulletins and integrated into training programs.

Crew members are encouraged to participate in safety meetings where recent incidents are discussed anonymously if necessary. This fosters an open environment where concerns can be raised without fear of reprisal. Regular audits will verify compliance with reporting quotas and the quality of submitted reports. Continuous feedback loops ensure that lessons learned translate into tangible improvements in shipboard operations and risk management.

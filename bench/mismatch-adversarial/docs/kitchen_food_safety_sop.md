# Galley Food Safety SOP

# Kitchen Food Safety Standard Operating Procedure

## Purpose and Scope
This document establishes mandatory safety protocols for all kitchen staff to ensure compliance with health regulations and protect customer well-being. Adherence to these standards is non-negotiable. Failure to follow these procedures may result in immediate corrective action or termination of employment. All staff must review this document annually and acknowledge receipt.

## Personal Hygiene
Strict personal hygiene practices are the first line of defense against foodborne illness. Staff must maintain high standards of cleanliness at all times while on duty.

- Hands are washed for at least 20 seconds using warm water and antibacterial soap. This protocol must be followed:
  - Before starting work.
  - After handling raw food items.
  - After using the restroom.
  - After touching hair, face, or body.
  - After handling waste or cleaning chemicals.
- Staff must wear clean uniforms and hats daily. Long hair must be fully contained under headgear.
- Open wounds on hands or arms must be covered with a waterproof bandage and a glove.
- Illness reporting is mandatory. Any staff member experiencing vomiting, diarrhea, or jaundice must not handle food and must inform management immediately.

## Temperature Control
Proper temperature management prevents the growth of pathogenic bacteria. All cooking, cooling, and holding processes must be monitored using calibrated thermometers.

- Cold food is stored at 5 C or below. This includes dairy products, cut fruits, vegetables, and prepared salads. Refrigerators must be checked twice daily to ensure they remain within this range.
- Hot food is held at 63 C or above. Buffets and hot holding units must maintain this temperature consistently. Food that drops below this threshold must be discarded if it has been in the danger zone for more than two hours.
- Cooking temperatures must reach safe internal levels based on the protein type, verified by checking the thickest part of the item.
- Cooling procedures must reduce food from 60 C to 21 C within two hours, and then from 21 C to 5 C or below within four additional hours.

## Cross-Contamination Prevention
Cross-contamination occurs when harmful bacteria are transferred from one surface or food item to another. Strict segregation protocols must be observed.

- Raw meat is stored on the bottom shelf of all refrigeration units. This placement prevents juices from dripping onto ready-to-eat foods, such as salads or cooked meats, stored on lower shelves.
- Separate cutting boards and knives must be used for different food categories. Chopping boards are colour coded: red for raw meat, green for vegetables. These tools must never be swapped between tasks.
- Ready-to-eat foods must be handled with gloves or utensils, never bare hands, after initial preparation.
- Allergen-containing ingredients must be stored in sealed containers clearly labeled with their contents to prevent accidental mixing during prep.

## Cleaning Schedule
A rigorous cleaning schedule ensures that the kitchen environment remains sanitary. Cleaning must be performed at specified intervals using approved food-safe sanitizers.

- High-touch surfaces, including door handles, light switches, and equipment controls, must be sanitized every two hours during service.
- Food preparation surfaces must be cleaned and sanitized between each different food task. For example, after chopping raw meat on a red board, the surface beneath it must be wiped down before preparing vegetables.
- Floor cleaning is required at the end of every shift. Spills must be addressed immediately to prevent slip hazards and bacterial growth.
- Deep cleaning of refrigerators, ovens, and exhaust hoods is conducted weekly by management or contracted specialists. Logs of these deep cleans must be kept on file for inspection.

## Allergen Management
Managing allergens is critical to preventing severe allergic reactions in customers. Transparency and separation are key components of this protocol.

- The 14 major allergens must be declared on the menu. This includes items such as peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, fish, crustaceans, mollusks, soy, wheat, celery, mustard, sesame, lupin, and sulphur dioxide.
- Staff must be trained to identify which menu items contain specific allergens. Any uncertainty must be resolved by consulting the chef or manager before serving.
- When preparing an order for a customer with allergies, dedicated utensils and clean surfaces must be used to avoid cross-contact with other ingredients.
- Orders containing allergens should be marked clearly in the kitchen ticketing system to alert all staff involved in preparation and plating.

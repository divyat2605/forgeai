# Assessment and Grading Handbook

This section outlines the formal procedures for evaluating student performance, determining final grades, and providing constructive feedback. Adherence to these guidelines ensures consistency across all modules and maintains the academic integrity of the university’s qualifications.

## Assessment Components

The assessment strategy for each module is designed to evaluate both continuous engagement with course material and comprehensive understanding at the end of the term. The total grade for every module is derived from two distinct components, weighted to reflect their respective contributions to the final outcome.

*   **Coursework (40%)**: This component assesses a student’s ability to apply theoretical knowledge to practical problems, conduct independent research, and communicate findings effectively. Coursework may take various forms, including essays, reports, presentations, or laboratory logs, depending on the specific requirements of the module.
*   **Examination (60%)**: The examination component tests the breadth and depth of a student’s knowledge under timed conditions. This ensures that students can demonstrate their understanding of core concepts and their ability to perform under pressure.

The weighting is fixed for all modules within this programme structure. Specifically, each module is assessed 40% by coursework and 60% by examination. Students must achieve a passing grade in the aggregate total; however, failure in one component may have specific implications depending on faculty regulations regarding minimum thresholds for individual parts.

## Grading Scale

The university employs a standard percentage-based grading scale to ensure clarity and comparability across different disciplines. The final module grade is calculated by combining the weighted scores from the coursework and examination components.

*   **Distinction**: This is the highest level of achievement, awarded to students who demonstrate exceptional mastery of the subject matter. A distinction requires an average of 70% or more.
*   **Pass**: The standard threshold for successful completion of a module. The module pass mark is 50%.
*   **Fail**: Any aggregate score below 50% results in a failing grade for the module.

Grades between these thresholds are awarded based on specific bandings defined in the faculty’s detailed grading policy, typically in increments of 5 or 10 percentage points. It is important to note that rounding rules apply to the final calculated average to determine the official recorded grade.

## Pass Marks

The pass mark serves as the critical benchmark for academic progression. For every module within this programme, the module pass mark is 50%. This means that a student must achieve an aggregate score of at least 50% across both coursework and examination components to be considered successful in that module.

Failure to meet this threshold results in a fail grade, which may impact the student’s progression to subsequent years or their eligibility for graduation. Students are advised to monitor their performance throughout the semester, as early intervention is often possible if academic difficulties arise.

## Resits

Students who fail a module are given one opportunity to retake the assessment. The resit policy is designed to provide a fair chance for improvement while maintaining academic standards.

*   **Eligibility**: Students may resit a failed module once. This applies to both the coursework and examination components if required by faculty regulations.
*   **Mark Capping**: To preserve the integrity of the first attempt, resit marks are capped at 50%. This means that even if a student performs exceptionally well in the resit assessment, the maximum grade they can receive for that module is 50%, which is sufficient to pass but will not contribute to a distinction classification.
*   **Procedure**: Resit assessments are typically scheduled during the summer term or at the beginning of the following academic year, depending on the faculty’s calendar.

## Feedback

Timely and constructive feedback is essential for student development. The university is committed to providing detailed comments that help students understand their strengths and areas for improvement.

*   **Turnaround Time**: Feedback on coursework is returned within 15 working days of the submission deadline. This ensures that students have sufficient time to review comments before the next assessment or module begins.
*   **Content**: Feedback includes both summative comments on the overall quality of the work and formative comments highlighting specific areas for development. Students are encouraged to engage with this feedback to enhance their future performance.

Students who believe there has been an error in marking or feedback provision may follow the formal academic appeals process outlined in the student handbook.

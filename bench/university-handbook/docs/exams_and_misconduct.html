Examinations and Academic Misconduct Handbook

Exam Conduct

Strict adherence to scheduling protocols is required to maintain the integrity of the examination process. Students are mandated to arrive at least 15 minutes before the exam start to allow time for seat allocation and material verification. Late arrivals disrupt the environment for all candidates and compromise security; therefore, no entry after 30 minutes of the scheduled start time will be permitted under any circumstances.

Students must present valid identification upon entry. Any attempt to bring unauthorized materials into the examination hall, including mobile phones, smartwatches, or personal notes, constitutes a severe breach of conduct. These items must be switched off and stored in designated areas outside the exam room. Candidates are expected to remain in their assigned seats for the duration of the assessment unless explicit permission is granted by invigilators for medical or emergency reasons.

Calculators and Materials

To ensure fairness across all disciplines, the use of electronic devices is strictly regulated. Only calculators from the approved list (Casio FX-83 and FX-85 series) are allowed during examinations. Any other model, including graphing calculators with memory functions or programmable devices not on this specific list, will be confiscated if discovered during the pre-exam check.

Students bringing unauthorized calculators must surrender them immediately to the invigilator. Failure to comply may result in the cancellation of their exam privileges for that session. All other writing materials must be clear and unmarked; highlighters with ink leaks or pens with decorative casings are prohibited if they obscure text or distract other candidates. Students should bring spare batteries if using a calculator, as replacements will not be provided by the institution.

Plagiarism

Plagiarism is defined as presenting work that is not your own without appropriate acknowledgment. This includes copying from textbooks, websites, other students, or previous assignments submitted by others. The institution utilizes advanced software to detect unoriginal content. Similarity reports above 25% are reviewed by the module leader to determine if the overlap constitutes plagiarism or acceptable citation.

During this review process, the module leader will analyze the context of the matched text. If it is determined that the student has failed to cite sources correctly or has paraphrased too closely without attribution, academic misconduct procedures will be initiated. It is the responsibility of every student to understand proper referencing styles for their specific field of study. Ignorance of citation rules is not accepted as a defense against plagiarism charges.

Use of AI Tools

The integration of artificial intelligence in academic work requires transparency and explicit permission. Generative AI tools may be used only where the assignment brief explicitly allows it. In such cases, students must provide a clear declaration of how the tool was utilized, including prompts used and outputs generated, as part of their submission.

Using AI to generate core arguments, analysis, or conclusions without permission is considered academic dishonesty. Students are expected to use AI for brainstorming, editing, or coding assistance only if stated in the brief. Failure to declare the use of these tools when permitted, or using them when prohibited, will be treated as a violation of academic integrity policies. The burden of proof lies with the student to demonstrate that their work is primarily their own intellectual effort.

Penalties

Academic misconduct carries significant consequences designed to uphold the value of qualifications awarded by the institution. A first offence of plagiarism normally results in a mark of zero for the assessment. This penalty applies regardless of whether the offense was intentional or negligent.

For more severe cases, such as organized cheating during exams or repeated offenses, the penalties may escalate to failure in the entire module, suspension from the program, or expulsion. Students have the right to appeal any decision regarding academic misconduct through the formal appeals process outlined in the student handbook. However, appeals must be based on procedural errors or new evidence, not on disagreement with the findings of fact. Maintaining honesty is essential for personal development and professional reputation.
